# The factory

How the factory carries an issue to a merged pull request, in detail. [The README](../README.md#watch-it-take-an-issue) tells the story and lists the commands people steer it with, and [SPEC.md](../SPEC.md) is the whole current state.

## Modeled proposals

Modeled behavior forks carry [checked previews for every option](statecharts.md#modeled-alternatives). Each isolated candidate model, sibling includes, statements, faithful bounds and pure view runs the ordinary model and reader checks with pinned TLC. Caller-owned prior choices, authorized revisions and parent context are staged before the agent; tool checks and the independent final check use that authority. One check reports every independent option failure within the shared allowance, retaining successful siblings for repair but publishing only a complete validated set.

A decision request shows all option words, literal-safe Mermaid and complete reports together, labeled unratified and model-only with candidate rules. Choosing previews neither ratifies rules nor starts a build. The complete source and evidence survive saved results, previous drafts, marker recovery and partial answers; remaining options keep their original checked context until a new draft is checked. Successful formalizations export per-option preview snapshots and model-only receipts with structured reader JSON and native script-free SVG. Reused exports remove stale alternatives, and selected-project verification computes its own chart.

New modeled publication rejects missing, swapped or stale evidence. Requests carrying previews are indivisible and must fit both the comment limit and the existing 4 MiB expanded marker limit; refusals carry no oversized payload. A post reporting a failed check keeps its proposal in the marker only while both limits hold, and otherwise leaves it to the saved run. Preview-less comments keep their existing wording, clipping and closing instructions. Plumbing and PRD forks remain chart-free, as do authenticated `kind: "step_fit"` questions asking only whether to build the complete step as one change or stop its parent and request a replacement plan on a new issue. This preserves D-0328.

New modeled proposals, revisions and amendments include [reader statecharts](statecharts.md) computed from the draft's exact model, sibling TLA+ includes, pure `view.json`, statements and checking bounds. The check tool reports view failures with concrete states, actions and hiding-rule IDs for repair. The independent final check recomputes from the final files, ignoring agent-supplied derived artifacts. The ordinary model checks still have to pass.

[Reader comparisons](statecharts.md#proposal-comparisons) accompany statement and bound diffs. New-project revisions compare with the preceding selected proposal; amendments resolve and capture their ratified project once, retaining `Target.baseline` across revisions, answers and recovery. Both check paths use detached caller-owned sources. A legacy baseline needs `baseline/view.json`, validated at its original bounds through model checks, complete exploration, invariant replay and hiding checks. Edits to staged baseline sources or locks cannot change that authority.

Comments and markers retain the complete candidate, named baseline, exact model/include/view/lock identities, hidden occurrence changes and visible added/removed states and arrows. View changes and differing bounds qualify interpretation; equal charts claim no behavioral equivalence. Missing or stale required comparisons block publication. The complete comparison remains subject to the existing indivisible comment and marker limits, with statement warnings, bound tables and ratification hashes preserved. The captured comparison is written to `.invariant/comparison.json` and protected through synthesis; histories are not recursively copied. [D-0362](../decisions/journal/D-0362.jsonl) records #648's comparison policy, and the merger runs its [pinned-TLC integration](statecharts.md#proposal-comparisons) when Docker is unavailable to the builder.

Charts are drawn for models of up to 500 reachable states. Above that capacity, passing checks save a successful `too_large` limitation and publication says how many states TLC reached at the recorded bounds, more than the 500 charts are drawn for. A well-formed view is still required, but its mappings and hiding are not evaluated. Bounds guidance stays “thousands of states, not millions”; models need not shrink to obtain a chart.

The chart shows initial states and computed arrows, with literal labels, exact rule wording, unavailable actions, hidden steps and separate TLC and chart counts. The size note keeps the statement or amendment table, existing checks and ratification instructions. A separate fingerprint binds all reader inputs, pinned tools and chart or limitation contents without changing statement hashes, locks or proof claims. Exact views, includes and evidence survive markers, recovery, previous drafts and amendments, and ratification writes `.invariant/view.json`, `.invariant/reader.json` and the sibling includes into the project. Older stored proposals remain readable, but missing or stale reader evidence refuses new publication, including after recovery. An oversized modeled comment is explicitly refused instead of truncating it while offering ratification. Publication also checks that the saved marker can be read back within its existing 4 MiB expanded limit, even when compression makes it fit in the comment. A refusal carries no oversized proposal payload. Plumbing and plans of issues remain chart-free.

This delivers [#603's ratified plan](../.invariant/plans/issue-603.json). [D-0345](../decisions/journal/D-0345.jsonl) records its proposal-view, artifact-binding and publication policy, including the nonblocking size limitation.

## Plumbing builds

Before proposing a built-in Go plumbing plan for a repository with `decisions/journal`, both the planner's check tool and the factory's final proposal check run the existing journal rebuild and decision-graph check on a copy of the base, after every acceptance source has been staged. Each check uses a fresh temporary store, leaving the original checkout, draft and machine's decision store unchanged. A journal rebuild failure or any graph problem refuses the draft with the checker's diagnostics, including unknown decision mentions in acceptance sources, untraced SPEC bullets and a stale journal log. Recorded decision mentions and fixture IDs assembled at run time remain eligible when their acceptance tests fail. Repositories without a journal keep their existing checks, and all acceptance failure-evidence rules below still apply ([#529's ratified plan](../.invariant/plans/issue-529.json), [D-0096](../decisions/D-0096-decision-graph.md), [D-0097](../decisions/journal/D-0097.jsonl), [D-0101](../decisions/journal/D-0101.jsonl), [D-0103](../decisions/journal/D-0103.jsonl), [D-0105](../decisions/D-0105-factory-takes-every-change.md), [D-0107](../decisions/journal/D-0107.jsonl), [D-0320](../decisions/journal/D-0320.jsonl)).

For built-in Invariant plumbing, the factory runs formatting, vet, the tests of every touched package and the pinned acceptance tests. Configured callers run their declared part checks and native selectors. A second agent reviews the whole candidate against the original checkout. When review asks for changes, the same builder receives the findings in its existing workspace under the unchanged ratified plan. It gets at most two repair attempts, each followed by fresh factory checks and, only if those pass, a fresh read-only review. Final passing checks and approval are required for readiness; configured trusted changes still stay drafts for a person. Unresolved findings leave a draft for a person, even when a repair fails tests or runs out of time; the failure post keeps both the findings and the check failures ([#281's ratified plan](../.invariant/plans/issue-281.json), [D-0105](../decisions/D-0105-factory-takes-every-change.md), [D-0108](../decisions/journal/D-0108.jsonl)).

The agent's test tool and the factory's checks use the same plumbing sandbox ([#397's ratified plan](../.invariant/plans/issue-397.json), [D-0281](../decisions/journal/D-0281.jsonl)). Invariant's built-in Go path compiles each touched package once, serially, before tests start, then reuses that binary from its package directory for discovery, tests and acceptance checks. Eight workers pull top-level tests, examples and fuzz seeds from one shared queue; subtests stay with their parent. Tests with no valid recorded duration come first, sorted by package and name, followed by known tests longest first, with ties in package/name order. Measured durations survive in the build's isolated shared Go cache for later checks. They are untrusted ordering hints: malformed or stale entries cannot add, omit or duplicate a discovered test, run commands or supply results. Each discovered test is queued exactly once for the touched-test phase, and pinned acceptance tests run again individually. Explicitly configured repository checks use the [execution API](#repository-check-execution) below.

Each watcher shares one queue across its plumbing builds' sandbox checks. `watch -check-parallel` defaults to 1; a larger machine can set any positive limit. This is independent of `-parallel`'s agent slots, three by default, and of agent execution: another build's agent, review or repair can run while a sandbox checks. The agent's test tool, even in a separate MCP process, and fresh factory checks after initial builds, fallbacks and repairs all join the same queue. Each admitted check keeps its place through sandbox cleanup, whether it succeeds, fails or is canceled. An unavailable queue fails the call. Stopping a build or watcher cancels its queued calls ([#458's ratified plan](../.invariant/plans/issue-458.json), [D-0303](../decisions/journal/D-0303.jsonl), [D-0105](../decisions/D-0105-factory-takes-every-change.md), [D-0113](../decisions/journal/D-0113.jsonl), [D-0173](../decisions/journal/D-0173.jsonl)).

While queued, the existing build's status and dashboard say **waiting for checks**, with the same run, agent, original start and completed checks. Admission restores **building**; neither transition records a completion. Waiting counts against the shared execution allowance for the initial build, fallback, checks, repairs and reviews. Only waits for an agent account's turn are excluded. If the build's own allowance expires while queued, its failed summary retains **waiting for checks**, including during an agent's MCP call. Queued plumbing jobs give tools their build allowance, rounded upward to the backend's units, within the clients' timer limits. An uncut build (`-timeout 0`) requests the longest safe backend timer instead of the ordinary fifteen-minute default: about 292 years for Codex and 2,147,483,647 milliseconds (about 24.85 days) for Claude Code. Queued calls disable Claude Code's separate idle timer, so silence in the queue cannot shorten that allowance. Claude Code's finite timer cap still applies; literally unbounded tool waits would need a different tool protocol. Ordinary non-gate jobs keep their existing defaults, and an explicitly larger Codex tool timeout is preserved ([D-0173](../decisions/journal/D-0173.jsonl), [D-0281](../decisions/journal/D-0281.jsonl)).

Under [#540's ratified plan](../.invariant/plans/issue-540.json), every modeled synthesis agent run also gives its gate tool the run's execution allowance through `Job.ToolTimeout`. Fallback refreshes it from the shared execution time remaining when present; ordinary synthesis keeps its existing fallback timeout restart policy. An uncut run uses the same negative value requesting the longest safe timer. Backend rounding, timer limits, larger explicit timeouts and account-wait accounting remain unchanged, and Claude Code's separate tool idle timer is disabled for these gates. This lets the gate spend its original allowance on TLC admission, cleanup and the [exclusive memory retry](gate.md#tlc-resource-budget). [D-0322](../decisions/journal/D-0322.jsonl) records this rule, which extends the queued plumbing timers above to modeled gates.

The sandbox uses the pinned Python image with the pinned Go toolchain and git, no network, a read-only module cache and hostname `localhost` ([D-0110](../decisions/journal/D-0110.jsonl), [D-0117](../decisions/journal/D-0117.jsonl), [D-0189](../decisions/journal/D-0189.jsonl)). Every container starts with Docker's `--init`, whose init process reaps orphaned children ([#426's ratified plan](../.invariant/plans/issue-426.json), [D-0290](../decisions/journal/D-0290.jsonl)). Its embedded runner uses only Python's standard library. Each container has a 4 GiB memory limit and a 1,024-process limit per admitted check. Compilation uses `GOMAXPROCS=2` and `-p=1`. Every process in it gets git configuration from its environment, `maintenance.auto=false` and `gc.auto=0` through `GIT_CONFIG_COUNT`, which git reads even when a test blanks its global and system config. A test's commit then starts no background maintenance that would write into its repository while its temporary directory is removed, and CI's jobs set the same ([D-0370](../decisions/journal/D-0370.jsonl)).

Built-in checks selecting the exact `internal/verify` package give the eight workers one shared twenty-minute touched-test phase inside a twenty-five-minute run ([#498's ratified plan](../.invariant/plans/issue-498.json), [D-0296](../decisions/journal/D-0296.jsonl)). Selecting it through an embedded asset or acceptance test counts too. Checks selecting only other packages keep ten minutes inside twelve, including a separate child package such as `internal/verify/pythongate`. Repository-contract checks retain their existing limits. The agent's test tool and fresh factory checks select the same budgets, whether direct or queued.

The whole-run deadline starts at queue admission, or entry for direct calls, including ownership discovery, checkout preparation, startup, formatting, vet, compilation, discovery and acceptance. Ownership discovery is bounded by the ordinary twelve-minute deadline; when it establishes the exception, the twenty-five minutes still count from entry or admission. Each test gets only the time left in the shared phase and the whole run. Neither another test nor a cold cache renews either allowance; overrides can only shorten them, and parent cancellation still applies. The one-minute startup reserve and fifteen-second cleanup reserve bring ordinary checks' delivery budget to thirteen minutes fifteen seconds, within their unchanged 900-second tool timer. Direct builds that can select `internal/verify` request twenty-six minutes fifteen seconds for tools, including when permitted deletions can leave it owning a child package's paths. Queued builds retain their existing tool allowances; queue waits and check execution still consume the build's shared allowance across builds, fallbacks, repairs and reviews ([D-0281](../decisions/journal/D-0281.jsonl), [D-0303](../decisions/journal/D-0303.jsonl), [D-0173](../decisions/journal/D-0173.jsonl)). Per-build caches keep their existing behavior. [D-0313](../decisions/journal/D-0313.jsonl) records the exception.

A deadline returns a failed report and removes the container. Reports retain package and test names for passed, failed, still-running and unstarted (`not_run`) work, even when the raw log is clipped. The summary names every test that didn't pass, first, and counts a passing run's tests instead of naming them; a run that didn't pass names at most 100 passed ones, so a large package's run fits an agent's tool result. A plan's pull request quotes at most 6,000 bytes of it ([D-0282](../decisions/journal/D-0282.jsonl)). Build failures, worker failures, timeouts and interruptions fail the check while independent work continues within the remaining time. Each acceptance test keeps its own result by package and name, including when neighbors fail or another package has the same test name. Before proposing a plan, its check rejects any acceptance test that already passes, any deadline or interruption, and any unfinished acceptance evidence. Only the testing package's own reports count as a test's result: text a test binary prints before its tests start, as a `TestMain` can, stays a diagnostic and leaves those tests unstarted. Completed failing tests and completed compiler failures in the acceptance test's package, such as a new API the base lacks, remain valid evidence that the code needs the change.

The opt-in real-image gate checks cold-run capacity on a cgroup-v2 Docker host, selecting `internal/verify` and its longer budget. Two simultaneous runs with fresh caches must both pass before twenty-five minutes; three must all return before twenty-six minutes fifteen seconds, with named partial failures allowed. Both waves check sampled working memory and kernel process peaks against the enforced limits, including compilation. Kernel memory peaks remain diagnostic. Image acquisition happens before the timed runs. Run it with `INVARIANT_PLUMBING_REAL=1 go test ./internal/plumbing -run '^TestSandboxBudgetAndRealCheckout$' -count=1 -timeout 70m`. Its ordinary and verify budget assertions also run without Docker; the real-image check returns normally when it is not enabled.

### Sandbox resource measurements

Under [#700's ratified plan](../.invariant/plans/issue-700.json), step 2 of #599, the opt-in budget observer samples cgroup-v2 files inside each container every **100 ms**, starting before the workload's preparation and ending with a final observation. Each working-memory sample is the sum of `anon` and `shmem` from **one read of `memory.stat`**. The reported peak is the largest of those sums, not the sum of separately observed maxima. A sampled sum at or above `memory.max`, any `memory.events` `oom` or `oom_kill`, a `pids.peak` at or above `pids.max`, or any denied process fails the resource check. Missing, malformed or unavailable required counters or sampling evidence also fail it. `memory.peak` includes file cache and is diagnostic: file-cache saturation and `memory.events` `max` events alone do not fail the check. The enforced limits are still 4 GiB, 1,024 processes and the configured CPU quota, four CPUs by default.

Sampling can miss a spike between observations. The working-memory peak is **not a kernel high-water mark**, and the nominal 100 ms interval is subject to scheduling and counter-read time. Phase transitions and finalization take additional bounded observations. Kernel `memory.peak` and `pids.peak` and cumulative OOM/process counters retain their distinct meanings. The sampler reads local cgroup files; it does not start a Docker command for every sample.

One host JSON report named by the full container ID is atomically updated during work and retained through success, failure, cancellation and forced removal. A separate bounded final Docker exec is attempted before removal if no healthy completed report exists. Removal is always delegated, including when observation or saving fails. Earlier sampled peaks, sample counts, cumulative counters, phase evidence and observer failures survive merges; a later healthy read cannot clear a sampling failure. An incomplete periodic snapshot cannot substitute for a failed final observation. Only a late removal after a valid completed report leaves that report unchanged. The report fields are:

| Fields | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `container_id`, `container_name` | Docker identity, also logged beside the real sandbox's outcome and slow tests |
| `working_memory_peak`, `memory_samples`, `sample_interval_seconds` | Maximum contemporaneous anon-plus-shmem sum in bytes, successful memory reads, nominal interval `0.1` |
| `memory_peak`, `memory_limit`, `memory_max_events` | Kernel peak in bytes, enforced `memory.max`, diagnostic `memory.events` `max` |
| `oom`, `oom_kill`, `pids_peak`, `pids_limit`, `pids_denied` | Cumulative OOMs/kills, kernel process peak, enforced process limit and denied processes |
| `cpu_max`, `cpu_stat` | Raw quota/period and all CPU counters, including `usage_usec`, `user_usec`, `system_usec`, `nr_periods`, `nr_throttled`, `throttled_usec` and additional kernel counters |
| `elapsed_seconds`, `complete`, `error` | Container observation wall time, successful final observation flag, optional sticky observer failure; a completed report with an error still fails |
| `phases` | Reached phases, each with `working_memory_peak`, `memory_samples`, `elapsed_seconds` and all observed `cpu_stat` deltas from that phase's start |

The runner emits `phase_start` records before `prepare` (inventory, formatting and vet), `build` (compilation and discovery), `test` (the shared queue) and `accept` (independent pinned reruns). Preparation sampling also covers entry before the runner starts. A phase not reached need not appear. CPU deltas and wall time cover phase boundaries; they are diagnostics and do not change verdicts. `Result.TestResults` retains each measured `elapsed_seconds` with package, test, phase, worker and acceptance file identities; absence differs from measured zero. Durations come from test lifecycle evidence, independently of cached ordering hints. The real budget log lists at most 20 slowest completed top-level tests per sandbox across test and acceptance phases, with package, test, phase and duration. Ordinary summaries remain compact.

For diagnosis, first inspect the wave's verdicts and named unfinished tests, then correlate the sandbox's container identity with its resource report. Compare preparation/build wall times and CPU deltas with the test and acceptance phases; inspect the slow-test list and throttling counters before attributing elapsed time to execution, contention or compilation. Compare sampled working memory with kernel peak and max events to distinguish working-memory pressure from cache-inclusive accounting. OOMs and denied processes remain failures even if the final usage is low. The report's elapsed time covers in-container observation; `Sandbox.Run` wall time also includes host preparation and startup. Neither includes image acquisition, which is logged separately.

## Container CPU limits

Under [#622's ratified plan](../.invariant/plans/issue-622.json), workload containers launched with Docker `run` or `create` default to **4 CPUs each**. Set `INVARIANT_CONTAINER_CPUS` in the host environment to change that default. Unset or empty means `4`; finite decimal counts of at least `0.01`, such as `0.5`, `2.5` or `8`, are accepted. Signs, exponents, nondecimal notation, whitespace, nonfinite values and smaller counts are refused with an error naming the setting before workload launch or its image acquisition. Cleanup remains available with an invalid setting.

The shared Docker runner, configured dependency installation, one-shot checks and persistent check environments use the same setting. Explicit container CPU limits take precedence, including the Python application executor's **1 CPU**, 512 MiB and 128-process limits. Arguments after the image remain workload arguments. Memory and process limits, isolation, image acquisition, cleanup, eight sandbox workers, check concurrency and production deadlines retain their existing behavior. The quota is per container, not an aggregate host budget: two default containers can consume eight CPUs and three can consume twelve, subject to the daemon's capacity. Docker-managed image-build workers, including BuildKit workers, are outside this `run`/`create` policy; configure their resources through Docker separately.

Both coding-agent backends explicitly pass the host setting, even when empty, in their MCP server environments alongside the existing tool allowance. Set the same value in the service environments for the watcher and dashboard and in the shell used for CLI checks. After changing it, [drain and restart the watcher](#draining-and-restarting), restart the dashboard and start new CLI and MCP processes. Changing a shell export does not update an already running service or container. For example, `export INVARIANT_CONTAINER_CPUS=4` selects the default explicitly before launching those processes.

The real-image budget observer also checks cgroup-v2 `cpu.max` against this setting and reports the complete `cpu.stat`, including usage and throttling microseconds, alongside memory/process peaks and elapsed time. Throttling is measured, not treated as a test failure; the existing pass and deadline requirements still decide the result. The two timeout fixtures use a fifteen-second shared phase inside a ninety-second fixture run; the interrupted test blocks until canceled and all 128 queue tests sleep for three seconds. The acceptance reruns add three seconds of process startup while retaining healthy completion, named interrupted work, independent acceptance and unstarted queued work. These are fixture allowances only.

## Repository check contract

Version 1 of `.invariant/repository.json` describes a repository's Go, Python and TypeScript parts, their checks, their owners and the files that supply check authority ([#461's ratified plan](../.invariant/plans/issue-461.json), [D-0301](../decisions/journal/D-0301.jsonl)). The shared `internal/repository` loader captures this contract from a caller-supplied committed base. It executes no declared commands. Plumbing on external repositories remains disabled ([D-0121](../decisions/journal/D-0121.jsonl)). Invariant continues to use its built-in plumbing runner, commands, sandbox limits and scope rules without requiring this file.

The top-level fields are `version`, `parts`, `agent_rules`, `current_state`, `trusted_paths` and `shared_paths`. `parts` is nonempty; each part has a unique `name` and `directory`, one supported `language`, an `image` pinned by `@sha256:` and a 64-digit hexadecimal digest, explicit `lockfiles` and `inputs` arrays, and a `commands` object. All paths are relative to the repository root, including a part's dependency inputs. Commands are defined to run in the part's directory. This example uses an illustrative image digest; a repository supplies its own toolchain image:

```json
{
  "version": 1,
  "parts": [
    {
      "name": "api",
      "directory": "api",
      "language": "go",
      "image": "registry.example/toolchain@sha256:aaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaa",
      "lockfiles": ["api/go.sum"],
      "inputs": ["api/go.mod"],
      "commands": {
        "install": {"argv": ["go", "mod", "download"]},
        "format": {"argv": ["./tools/check-format"]},
        "lint": {"argv": ["go", "vet", "./..."]},
        "build": {"argv": ["go", "build", "./..."]},
        "test": {"argv": ["go", "test", "./..."]},
        "test_one": {"argv": ["go", "test", "./...", "-run", "^{test}$", "-count=1"]}
      }
    }
  ],
  "agent_rules": ["AGENTS.md"],
  "current_state": ["SPEC.md", "README.md"],
  "trusted_paths": ["tools/"],
  "shared_paths": [{"path": "shared/", "parts": ["api"]}]
}
```

`language` is `go`, `python` or `typescript`, and a repository may mix them. Each of the six command entries is required. It holds either a nonempty `argv` array with a nonblank executable, or a nonblank reason such as `{"not_applicable": "This application has no distribution artifact."}`, never both. `lockfiles` and `inputs` are required arrays but may be empty. The executable form of `test_one` contains exactly one `{test}` placeholder in one argument: for example, `"^{test}$"` with Go's `-run`, `"{test}"` as a pytest selector, or `"{test}"` after `--testNamePattern` with direct `vitest run` or `jest`. This is literal command data, with no shell interpolation by the loader or execution API. An inapplicable `test_one` needs an explicit reason like any other check. The example's `api/tools/check-format` must be an executable script that exits nonzero for formatting differences: `gofmt -l` alone only prints differences and cannot fail a command check.

The loader rejects unsupported versions and languages, mutable image tags without a digest, malformed digests, unknown or duplicate JSON keys, trailing JSON, missing required fields and contradictory commands. Part directories must exist in the base commit, and agent rules, current-state documents, lockfiles and inputs must be regular files there. Paths use `/`, are nonempty and clean, and stay inside the repository; only a part's `directory` may be `.`. Backslashes, control characters, Git metadata, globs, symlink ancestors or leaves, and gitlinks are refused. Unrelated links elsewhere in the repository do not invalidate a contract.

`trusted_paths` entries and each `shared_paths.path` select an exact file or a directory prefix ending in `/`; prefixes can reserve future files. A shared rule lists its owners in `parts`. Duplicate or overlapping shared selectors, duplicate part names or directories, and unknown owners are invalid. Nested part directories are allowed.

For each touched file, ownership is the deepest enclosing part, plus any explicitly shared owners and every part declaring that file as a lockfile or input. A root part therefore owns files outside its nested parts without also owning their code. A workspace lockfile can select several parts, even outside their directories. Changes to `.invariant/repository.json`, `agent_rules` or `current_state` files select every part. Selection returns unique parts sorted by name, independent of declaration order. New paths need not exist, deleted paths retain their base ownership, and any unowned or unsafe path fails the entire selection. An empty set of touched files selects no parts.

Ownership and trust answer different questions. Trust includes CI and the gate workflow (`.github/`, `.gitlab-ci.yml` and `.circleci/`), the contract itself, root and nested `AGENTS.md` and the `AGENTS.override.md` Codex reads in its place, `CLAUDE.md` and `CLAUDE.local.md`, `.codex/`, `.claude/` and `.mcp.json`, declared agent rules, every declared lockfile and input, and `trusted_paths`. Ordinary code and current-state documents are not implicitly trusted. Agent file names match whatever their case, since a case-insensitive file system, as macOS's, serves `app/agents.md` to an agent asking for `app/AGENTS.md`; otherwise path components match exactly: `AGENTS.md.bak` and `.mcp.json.example` gain no implicit trust. A symbolic link at a trusted path, say `CLAUDE.md -> docs/notes.md`, makes what it names trusted too, since an edit there changes what every agent reading the link sees ([D-0381](../decisions/journal/D-0381.jsonl)). The loader resolves each such link through the base tree, never the candidate, following chains, linked directories and links inside a linked directory. A target that doesn't exist yet is trusted all the same, because adding it would bring the link to life, and a link to the repository's root trusts everything. A link that leaves the repository or loops names nothing, and scope already refuses any change through a link, the link itself included. New and deleted paths use the same captured policy. For Invariant itself, the loader under `internal/repository/` and `.invariant/repository.json` are also in its built-in trusted base, so their changes require a person to merge them ([D-0109](../decisions/journal/D-0109.jsonl)).

`repository.Load(ctx, checkout, baseRef)` requires an explicit nonempty commit or ref and resolves it once. The resulting snapshot exposes the resolved commit through `Commit()`, independent configuration through `Config()`, committed file bytes through `ReadFile(path)` or the cancellable `ReadFileContext(ctx, path)`, applicable parts through `PartsFor(paths)`, and the captured trust policy through `IsTrusted(path)`. `DirectoryOwner(path) (Part, bool, error)` returns an independent copy of the deepest enclosing part, or false for a safe path without one. It respects directory component boundaries and a root part as fallback, for existing, new and deleted paths. `PartsFor` uses the same directory rule and retains the additional shared, dependency and global-document selection rules above. A global document never hides another unsafe or unowned touched path.

Every method stays bound to that commit. Changing the index, working tree, candidate configuration or base ref, or mutating returned configuration, parts or bytes, cannot replace its authority. `ErrMissing` means only that the contract is absent from a valid base tree; invalid configuration returns `ErrInvalid`, and invalid revisions and read failures never masquerade as missing configuration. File reads return `ErrFileMissing` only for a safe file path absent from the captured tree. Unsafe paths, nonregular entries, cancellation and unreadable committed objects do not match it. Callers use `errors.Is` to distinguish absence through wrapped errors.

Successful committed blob reads, including empty files and the contract itself, are cached by blob ID within each immutable snapshot. Concurrent callers receive independent bytes; each caller's cancellation and deadline apply to both hits and reads. Failed reads are not cached, and a new snapshot has its own cache. These APIs and reuse follow [#661's ratified plan](../.invariant/plans/issue-661.json), preserving the captured authority of [D-0301](../decisions/journal/D-0301.jsonl) and native identity of [D-0314](../decisions/journal/D-0314.jsonl). [D-0361](../decisions/journal/D-0361.jsonl) records the snapshot API and cache.

The loader has no automatic fallback to Invariant's built-in policy or to a modeled project. Modeled projects continue to load their own `.invariant/invariant.json` and ratified locks unchanged, with or without a repository contract. The loader supplies the shared contract and ownership queries; a trusted caller can pass its snapshot to the execution API below. Loading alone executes nothing and does not relax external-plumbing, sandbox or scope restrictions. The contract's decision is [D-0301](../decisions/journal/D-0301.jsonl).

## Repository dependency preparation

[#468's ratified plan](../.invariant/plans/issue-468.json) supplies a Go API in `internal/plumbing`: `DependencyPreparer{CacheDir, Platform, Timeout, Runner}.Prepare(ctx, snapshot, partNames)` returns a `DependencySession`. A nil runner uses Docker. `Artifacts()` returns independent copies of verified environment evidence; `Run(ctx, partName, candidateRoot, argv)` checks a candidate offline in that part's prepared environment. The caller supplies an explicit Linux platform (`linux/amd64` or `linux/arm64`, with an explicit variant where needed) and a cache directory outside candidate control. The execution API below uses this preparation for configured builders; there is no candidate-controlled CLI configuration or external-plumbing intake. Invariant keeps its existing read-only module cache and per-build compilation cache, and the modeled Python application gate is unchanged.

Preparation selects only the named parts of the captured contract. Repeated names count once; an empty selection does no work. Unknown names and attempts to run unprepared parts fail. Every selected declaration is admitted before installation begins. Dependency bytes come from the captured blobs, using cancellable reads, even if a candidate changes its manifest or lockfiles or another commit becomes `HEAD`.

| Part | Admitted installation and inputs | Enforced policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Go | Exactly `go mod download`; `go.mod` in `inputs`, `go.sum` in `lockfiles` with module and go.mod SHA-256 sums covering every required version | Local pinned toolchain, readonly modules, workspace and user Go configuration disabled, public module proxy without direct VCS fallback |
| Python | `python` or `python3 -m pip install`, with `-r` or `--requirement` for each declared requirements lockfile; entries use `name==version` and one or more `--hash=sha256:` values | Private virtual environment; interpreter isolation (`-I`) for both venv creation and pip; hash checking with `--ignore-installed`, binary wheels only, no user configuration, interactive input or pip version check |
| TypeScript | `npm ci`; `package.json` in `inputs`, `package-lock.json` v2/v3 in `lockfiles`, matching root declarations and a complete resolved dependency graph with HTTPS artifacts and SHA-256/512 integrity | Lifecycle scripts, audit and funding disabled; private npm cache and distinct empty user and global configuration files outside the repository subtree |

Ordinary npm registry ranges, including caret, tilde, wildcard, comparator and alternative ranges, are accepted when the lock resolves them consistently. All installed Python transitive dependencies must be listed with exact versions and hashes; pip's resolver enforces that during installation. Requirements may continue hash lists with backslashes and contain comments. Pip accepts the mandatory safety flags and `--quiet`/`-q`; npm accepts its mandatory safety flags and `--quiet`. The adapter adds the safety flags even when the declaration omits them.

Admission rejects duplicate JSON properties and case variants of the npm fields it validates, so it cannot check a different value from the one npm uses. Python interpreter isolation keeps staged inputs such as `pip.py` and `venv.py` from replacing the installer modules. Pip ignores installed packages so even a declared bootstrap package such as pip itself must be installed from an artifact verified against the declared hashes.

Unsupported forms fail explicitly: shell wrappers and other package managers; Go replacements, exclusions and workspaces; Python floating or wildcard versions, editable installs, direct references, markers and recursive requirements or constraints; npm workspaces, links, aliases, bundled packages, overrides, shrinkwrap and local or VCS artifacts. Implicit manager configuration such as `.npmrc`, `pip.conf` and `go.work` is refused among dependency inputs. The contract loader still accepts inert declarations independently of this narrower preparation policy. Go modules without requirements need no go.sum; empty Python requirements and npm locks with only the root package also work. `install.not_applicable` normally requires empty `inputs` and `lockfiles`; captured native Go additionally permits only `go.mod` with no required modules and an optional empty `go.sum`, retaining and verifying those bytes in its offline preparation recipe; Docker seals the pinned environment offline without running a repository installation command.

Only declared dependency inputs are staged, as regular files at their repository-relative paths. Docker copies them into private container storage and starts the installation in the part's directory. Installation and check stages reach `docker cp -` as tar streams whose files and directories have UID and GID zero. This lets a watcher running as an ordinary host user use the existing root containers with all capabilities dropped and `no-new-privileges`, while retaining file contents and ordinary permissions. No checkout, credentials, host cache or Docker socket is mounted. The pinned toolchain image must supply `/bin/sh`, `/usr/bin/env`, basic filesystem utilities and its language tools on the standard system paths; Python also needs `venv`. Images declaring volumes are refused because their mutable contents would not be sealed by an image commit. Network access and any acquisition of the pinned base image belong only to preparation. An unchanged-input check reads back the container's copies after a successful install. The host then seals recipe evidence in the image configuration, independently of repository files.

Cache records identify an immutable environment image and resolved immutable base, platform, recipe and every input hash. The key covers all declared inputs' paths, roles and bytes, the language, part directory, exact install arguments or inapplicable reason, pinned image, platform, effective installation policy and versioned adapter recipe. Commit IDs, checkout paths and part names are not keys, so identical dependencies in another checkout reuse the same environment. Every publication and reuse independently inspects image identity, platform, base ancestry and sealed evidence. Missing, corrupt or mismatched evidence is refused. A failed installation publishes nothing even if it leaves complete-looking output. Concurrent creators may install separately in private containers; atomic record replacement exposes only verified complete output.

Checks use the immutable image ID with `--network none`, `--pull never`, an overridden entrypoint, explicit environment, suppressed Docker proxy defaults, `--init`, 4 GiB of memory and a 1,024-process limit. The adapter passes the argument array without shell interpolation. Candidate files are copied into disposable container storage, preserving ordinary permissions, including executable bits, while stripping special permission bits, refusing links and excluding Git metadata. Dependency writes and compilation caches stay in that private container; a check cannot poison another session or change the original checkout or shared cache records. Canceling a check removes its container and preserves the verified dependency image.

The deadline is fixed at `Prepare` entry: at most `SandboxTimeout` (twelve minutes), or a shorter `Timeout` or parent deadline. Input reads, staging, all cold installations, cache access and later checks share it. No part, cache miss or later check gets fresh time; checks also honor their own shorter context and the original parent's cancellation. Private containers are removed under `SandboxCleanupAllowance` (fifteen seconds), using a fresh bounded cleanup context even after cancellation, and temporary staging is removed.

Candidate staging reads directory entries in batches and copies regular files in bounded chunks, checking cancellation between operations. Each copy reads only the file's captured size and rejects a size or modification-time change; it never follows a continuously growing file to EOF. Opened directories and files must match the entries checked, with symlinks, hard links and special files refused.

The acceptance tests in `internal/plumbing/dependenc*_accept_test.go` cover locked and empty graphs, refusals, input capture, content-based reuse, independent evidence, concurrent creators, failed installs, cancellation, deadlines, candidate isolation and actual Docker launch arguments with the executable replaced. Additional regressions exercise transitive npm lock consistency, semver ranges and case aliases; distinct empty npm configurations; the captured Python installer script against staged shadow modules and matching bootstrap pip versions with valid and invalid wheel hashes; direct execution of executable and nonexecutable candidate scripts and removal of special permissions; cancellation during file copying and directory traversal; and Docker installation exit status, changed input refusal, immutable base ancestry and volume refusal. Installer checks use local Python with empty graphs or test wheels and, when available, npm with empty graphs. These run without network or a Docker daemon. The root-ownership acceptance test also inspects the adapter's actual Docker input stream for installation and checks, including ownership, bytes, permissions, input selection, Git metadata exclusion, link and special-file refusals, failed copies and cancellation.

The same test optionally prepares real Go, Python and TypeScript parts using the pinned toolchain images and one real dependency each (`github.com/google/uuid`, `packaging` and `is-number`). Each part installs once with network, tests its dependency offline, prepares again and tests the same immutable environment without another installation. The checks assert root ownership, offline networking and private writes that leave the checkout untouched. Run it as an ordinary host user with Docker access to cover the original ownership failure:

```bash
INVARIANT_PLUMBING_DEPENDENCIES_REAL=1 go test ./internal/plumbing -run '^TestRepositoryDependencyRootOwnedStagesAndRealPreparation$' -count=1 -timeout 45m
```

This needs Go at the repository's required version, Git, and a working Docker CLI and daemon supporting Linux containers on amd64 or arm64, with the existing 4 GiB memory and 1,024-process container limits. Network access must allow pulling the pinned images and fetching the Go module, Python wheel and npm package; the test also fetches the fixed npm version's integrity metadata before capturing its lockfile. Enabling the test makes unavailable Docker or failed preparation an error. It removes the environment images it creates and retains pinned base images. Real preparation remains merger verification when Docker or network access is unavailable ([#485's ratified plan](../.invariant/plans/issue-485.json), following [D-0305](../decisions/journal/D-0305.jsonl), [D-0310](../decisions/journal/D-0310.jsonl)).

## Repository check execution

[#484's ratified plan](../.invariant/plans/issue-484.json) adds `Sandbox.Repository *RepositoryChecks`. A trusted caller supplies `RepositoryChecks{Snapshot, Dependencies, Adapters}` and the sandbox's `Original` checkout. `Dependencies` is the preparer above; the optional host-selected `Adapters` support the [acceptance foundation](#configured-acceptance-foundation) below. A nil repository configuration keeps Invariant's existing Go selection, scheduling and caches. A configured sandbox with no snapshot fails; it never reloads the candidate's contract or falls back to built-in checks.

`Sandbox.Run(ctx, candidate, plan)` selects parts from the plan's files, acceptance files and new regressions admitted against `Original`, using the captured `PartsFor` rules. Generated plan-lock paths and unrelated candidate files do not widen selection. Shared inputs, additions and deleted paths retain their captured owners. Invalid ownership fails before dependency work. All selected dependencies are prepared before commands start.

The trusted Go host runs each selected part's captured `format`, `lint`, `build` and `test` commands in order, passing literal argument arrays in the part's directory. A nonzero exit fails any phase, including formatting; a formatter must be configured to fail on differences. An inapplicable phase is skipped with its captured reason. Parts run serially, with at most one candidate container active per admitted check. Each part keeps one disposable writable container across its applicable commands, preserving build outputs; an entirely inapplicable command list opens no container. Acceptance selectors use separate fresh containers as described under [invocation isolation](#configured-invocation-isolation). It uses the verified immutable dependency image, `--network none`, `--pull never`, an overridden entrypoint, explicit environment with proxy defaults suppressed, `--init`, 4 GiB and 1,024 processes. No host cache is mounted. The image needs `/bin/sh`, `/usr/bin/env` and `sleep` alongside its language tools; Go and Node images need no Python runner.

`DependencyPreparer.Runner` keeps its existing interface. For repository checks it also implements `OpenChecks(context.Context, DependencyRequest) (RepositoryCheckEnvironment, error)`; the default `DockerDependencyRunner` does so. The verified request has no command and cannot pull or enable networking. `RepositoryCheckEnvironment.Run(ctx, argv)` returns diagnostics and an error for unsuccessful execution. `Close(ctx)` removes the private container and its staged files; the caller must close after success, failure or cancellation using a fresh bounded context. Docker cleans up failed startup inside `OpenChecks`; the lifecycle also closes any environment returned alongside a startup error. Cleanup failures fail the check.

The whole-run deadline starts at `Sandbox.Run` entry, or queue admission, and includes selection, dependency preparation, copying, startup and every command. It is capped at twelve minutes and by the parent and any shorter override. The preparer's shorter allowance counts from that same entry, including selector resolution. The first suite test or acceptance staging starts one shared ten-minute test allowance; later parts' setup, commands and selectors consume its remaining time without renewal. No later invocation extends either deadline. The existing one-minute startup and fifteen-second cleanup reserves remain; cleanup uses an uncanceled context bounded to fifteen seconds, removes a container before its staging, and completes before the queue releases its slot.

`Result.CommandResults` retains the part, phase and status of every selected command: passed, failed, skipped, running or unstarted (`not_run`), with reasons for skipped and unfinished phases. A candidate command failure stops execution and retains completed work and diagnostics alongside all unfinished commands. The public planning and lower-level base paths continue after ordinary build and suite exits to collect independent acceptance failures. Host-side status decides results; command output is never parsed as result records, deadlines or acceptance passes. These are suite outcomes only. Go, pytest, Vitest and Jest use their default individual adapters; unsupported frameworks and configured legacy acceptance are refused before preparation. Neither suite success, setup failure nor the legacy `Accepted` map can qualify a draft or build. Invariant's built-in Go evidence path remains operational.

Initial execution, tool calls, final checks, fallback, repairs and review retain the same build allowance. ## Configured invocation isolation

Under [#621's ratified plan](../.invariant/plans/issue-621.json), every configured Go, pytest, Vitest or Jest acceptance invocation gets a fresh disposable container from the verified immutable prepared image and a new private copy of the pristine staged base or candidate. Discovery, helper compilation, selected execution and evidence collection all belong to that invocation. Captured policy, verbatim pinned sources and the existing authentic evidence rules apply on both paths.

A part's format, lint, build and suite commands still share their outputs in a separate container. Acceptance cannot consume their generated files, modified production, replaced tools or frameworks, startup hooks or writable caches. It starts from staged inputs and prepared dependencies, so any generation acceptance needs must happen within that invocation. An earlier selector cannot prepare a later selector's environment either. An entirely inapplicable command list opens no command container. Node dependencies are sealed before discovery inside each fresh acceptance environment and checked around that selector.

Invocations remain serial. The prior environment and its owned process trees close before staging is removed and another environment opens. Cleanup uses a bounded uncanceled fifteen-second context, retaining the queue slot; a failed close stops later selectors while keeping earlier authentic evidence. Partial startup is cleaned up, and startup failures also stop subsequent work. The first suite test or acceptance staging starts the single shared ten-minute test allowance. Staging, startup, discovery, helper work and runtime consume it; the original twelve-minute whole-run deadline never renews. External intake and the unconfigured Go runner keep their existing behavior.

The default isolation matrix covers all four configured adapters on base and candidate paths, with and without commands. A mandatory daemon-free control and a real-Go attack through the production Docker transport exercise modified production inputs and poisoned private caches. The offline real-image attacks actually replace Go/compiler or Python interpreter files, corrupt compiler/bytecode caches, replace pytest and install a Python startup hook. They require both the hostile first selector and independent later authentic evidence, then repeat after a production-only correction. No image or dependency is downloaded.

With Docker and already-local digest-pinned images containing Go 1.27.x and Python 3.13/pytest 8.3.5, run:

```bash
INVARIANT_ACCEPTANCE_ISOLATION_REAL=1 \
INVARIANT_ACCEPTANCE_ISOLATION_GO_IMAGE='local-go@sha256:<digest>' \
INVARIANT_ACCEPTANCE_ISOLATION_PYTEST_IMAGE='local-pytest@sha256:<digest>' \
INVARIANT_ACCEPTANCE_ISOLATION_PLATFORM=linux/arm64 \
go test ./internal/plumbing -run '^TestInvocationOffline(Images|Costs)621$' -count=1 -v -timeout 60m
```

Use the images' actual Linux platform. Enabling the opt-in makes missing Docker, images or pinned runtimes an error. `TestInvocationOfflineCosts621` compares two benign selectors in the former shared-container loop with the same selectors in fresh containers. Both arms use the same immutable prepared image, source bytes, selection, discovery, producer and collector. Three repeats alternate arm order and exercise both independent base failures and candidate passes. Every sample must have authentic evidence. Each comparison retains the ordinary whole-run and shared test deadlines; repetition is outside those runs.

The verbose report names the pinned and prepared image IDs, platform and runtime, followed by one row per invocation, mode and repeat:

| Cost | Measurement |
| --- | --- |
| `staging` | Host copy from the validated pristine input tree. Zero for a reused shared environment. |
| `startup` | `OpenChecks`, including its internal staging, root-owned Docker transfer, create and start. Zero for reuse. |
| `compilation` | Actual Docker `go build` and `go test -c` elapsed time, including discovery, instrumentation and transport helpers. Python has no separate compiler command; its imports and bytecode work stay in total. |
| `total` | Invocation wall time through evidence validation and applicable cleanup, including all the above costs. Shared-container cleanup is charged to its last selector. |

Sum the two invocation totals for each repeat to compare complete runs; compare the second selector's rows to see the loss of writable-cache reuse. Dependency preparation and the common initial source capture are excluded equally from both arms. No wall-time threshold is a correctness assertion, and daemon-free timings are not a substitute for real-image costs. Real-image measurements remain merger verification when Docker or those local images are unavailable to the builder.

This policy rests on [D-0108](../decisions/journal/D-0108.jsonl), [D-0311](../decisions/journal/D-0311.jsonl), [D-0316](../decisions/journal/D-0316.jsonl) and [D-0352](../decisions/journal/D-0352.jsonl). [D-0371](../decisions/journal/D-0371.jsonl) records #621's fresh-container policy on those grounds, with the merger's real-image measurements: a fresh Go invocation recompiles, about 5.2 s more per extra selector, and a pytest one costs about 0.2 s more.

## Native acceptance plans

[#503's ratified plan](../.invariant/plans/issue-503.json) extends plumbing acceptance records with explicit native identities. The schema keeps legacy Go records unchanged: `name`, `file`, `says`, with no inferred metadata or execution binding. Their canonical hash bytes and committed locks need no migration or repository contract. A native record supplies **all three** of `part`, `framework` and `selector`; absent, empty or null members of a partial native record are errors. Its `name` is a display label, so several identities may share it.

```json
{
  "name": "Worker readiness",
  "summary": "Require the selected worker case to be ready.",
  "files": ["worker/main.py"],
  "tests": [{
    "name": "ready",
    "part": "worker",
    "framework": "pytest",
    "file": "worker/test_ready.py",
    "selector": "test_ready.py::TestWorker::test_ready[case one]",
    "says": "The selected worker case is ready."
  }]
}
```

The draft puts each source under `tests/` at its exact repository path. Sources must be new regular UTF-8 files, reached through safe ancestors, without symbolic or hard links, writable-source overlap or extra source entries. Bytes, including CRLF and a missing final newline, are preserved. A repository's line-ending normalization, such as `text=auto`, may store a source with other line endings: staging and scope accept that copy, and the build's tests run the ratified bytes. The hash pins summary, writable files, each complete `(part, framework, file, selector)` identity, labels, assertions, source bytes and execution digest. File and test declaration order does not affect it; duplicate identities are refused even when their labels differ.

| Framework | Part language and file | Exact selector |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| `go` | `go`; `*_test.go` | One declared `TestX` function, optionally followed by `/` and the exact subtest path. The top-level function must be unambiguous in its package. Selectors are literal identities, never patterns, so the Go adapter matches them exactly. |
| `pytest` | `python`; `test_*.py` or `*_test.py` | The source path relative to the part directory, followed by `::function` or `::Class::method`, including a concrete parameter ID when needed. File-only and class-only selections are refused. |
| `vitest`, `jest` | `typescript`; `.test` or `.spec` files, or files under `__tests__/`, with a JS/TS extension (`js`, `jsx`, `mjs`, `cjs`, `ts`, `tsx`, `mts`, `cts`) | The complete nested test title, including a concrete parameterized case's title. Spaces and punctuation are literal. The file is independently part of the identity. |

Validation checks each selector's shape. Pytest collection must establish exactly one matching concrete item; parameter groups are refused even when they contain one case. Vitest and Jest discovery requires one literal full title in the exact file. Labels, parts and selectors may hold spaces and punctuation, but no control, format, private-use or line-separating character, so nothing invisible can hide or reorder the identity a person ratifies. A plan's records are all native or all legacy: nothing infers a part for a legacy record, so a mixed plan is refused.

The native file must execute in the directory returned by the captured snapshot's `DirectoryOwner`; ancestor, shared or dependency check owners cannot move it elsewhere, and a file with no enclosing part is refused. The part's language must match and its captured `test_one` must have executable arguments. Only Go sources undergo Go declaration checks. Python and JavaScript discovery uses the prepared framework through its factory-owned observer.

Under [#668's ratified plan](../.invariant/plans/issue-668.json), source display, file/selector validation, owner language, declaration dispatch and default adapter lookup share one definition for each of the four frameworks. JavaScript extensions still display as JavaScript in a TypeScript part. Structural admission establishes neither supported command arguments nor discovery, compilation or execution evidence; trusted adapter overrides and each default adapter's command and evidence rules remain unchanged. Non-Go source stays verbatim and receives no Go parsing. [D-0374](../decisions/journal/D-0374.jsonl) records this reuse; [D-0375](../decisions/journal/D-0375.jsonl) records that, with #621's fresh acceptance containers, a check opens its commands' container and one per acceptance invocation.

Go declarations share one analysis with explicit legacy and native policies. Legacy keeps its permissive structural rule: one parameter field pointing to any qualified `T`, without resolving its import or rejecting grouped parameter names, results, type parameters or duplicate function declarations. Native requires exactly one top-level function with one parameter pointing to the imported `testing.T` (including aliases and dot imports), no results and no type parameters. Its selector supplies the function name; its display label does not. Methods do not count under either policy. Package-wide ambiguity and full-identity checks remain separate. This analysis is not type checking, and changes no canonical hash bytes or committed locks.

`ReadDraft(workspace)` performs structural inspection. `ReadRepositoryDraft(ctx, workspace, snapshot)` additionally checks the trusted caller's captured repository contract and binds native plans to `execution: {"base": "<captured commit>", "digest": "sha256:<content digest>"}`. It accepts a new acceptance file only when `errors.Is(err, repository.ErrFileMissing)` identifies absence; existing files, unsafe paths and unreadable objects remain refusals, including after cached reads. Draft `plan.json` cannot supply `execution`, `sources` or `trusted`. Legacy plans acquire no execution field. `Plan.ValidateRepository(ctx, snapshot)` verifies the binding without rewriting it.

The execution digest keeps its version-1 canonical JSON encoding of the entire captured configuration, the exact bytes of the declared lockfiles and inputs of each part that runs a native test, and sorted planned paths and native identities. It uses the existing `dependencyHash` helper for the same `sha256:` encoding. Set-like declarations are sorted; command argument order is preserved. Repeated validation reuses the snapshot's captured bytes without new Git object reads. An unrelated commit, including one that changes only agent-rule, current-state document or another part's dependency bytes, or different JSON formatting, does not change authority. Changed policy or relevant input bytes in a new snapshot require a new proposal. The captured base remains provenance and is excluded from the ratified hash; verification at an identical contract on another commit leaves that provenance untouched. All native and legacy hashes and locks remain compatible. Candidate files and serialized digest claims never supply execution policy.

Saved proposals, revisions, ratification comments and locks retain full identities and verbatim source; builder prompts name each full identity and the captured digest. Dashboard state retains the identities and a full-hash source reference, with exact acceptance files available through the [shared disclosure](#dashboard-acceptance-sources). Comments and the dashboard show language-tagged sources and treat native labels as text. Configured planning owns a local check queue: the separate tool sends only the draft workspace, while the caller retains the original checkout and captured snapshot for both checks and final proposal creation. Build queue registrations retain the original plan and authority; child requests cannot replace either. Staging and scope keep the acceptance sources and selectors fixed against the original baseline through repairs. New regular regression files follow the captured test conventions described under [What a build may write](#what-a-build-may-write), including Python and TypeScript tests throughout a touched part.

Under [#702's ratified plan](../.invariant/plans/issue-702.json), native ratification tables render each label, assertion, part, framework, file and selector in an HTML code element, escaping the raw value exactly once with the shared receipt text encoder. Mentions, issue references, backticks, pipes, backslashes, HTML, entity-looking text, quotes and assertion line endings stay literal, with one table row per record and no automatic links or notifications from those values. Authored explanatory prose and command links, full execution digest and base provenance, and language-tagged verbatim source blocks keep their presentation. A nil registered plan denotes planning; a nonnil copied plan denotes building, even when both retain originals. Planning refuses build requests and building refuses replacement drafts. Registration still copies the adapter map and captured policy, and tool and final checks execute afresh against that authority. Shared budgets, cancellation and queue ownership through bounded cleanup retain their existing contracts.

Configured Go, Python and TypeScript planning and builds use the default Go, pytest, Vitest and Jest adapters and the lifecycle below. Unsupported frameworks and configured legacy acceptance return unstarted or refused results with full identities before dependency preparation, without migration or fallback to the built-in Go runner. ## Configured acceptance foundation

Selection validates the captured invocation form and supplies literal arguments and package attribution. The lifecycle validates the complete native plan, captured execution binding, ownership and every selection before preparation. All four frameworks have default adapters, preserving explicit trusted overrides; diagnostics never supply evidence. The lifecycle copies only regular files with safe ancestors into private staging, checks any existing acceptance source against the ratified bytes up to line-ending normalization, then stages the verbatim source. Dependencies are prepared and verified through the existing API; each part retains one disposable command environment, while every selector opens its own fresh environment and pristine source copy. Each closes before another workload opens.

## Go acceptance selection and discovery

[#527's ratified plan](../.invariant/plans/issue-527.json) adds the zero-value `GoRepositoryAcceptanceAdapter`. Captured native Go uses it by default; a trusted host may override `RepositoryChecks.Adapters["go"]`. Candidate configuration cannot select an adapter. `Select(ctx, part, test)` validates the captured `test_one` command before staging, dependency preparation or workload execution and returns `RepositoryAcceptanceSelection{Package, Argv}`. The preliminary package is part-relative, and the returned argument slice is independent of the captured command.

Supported commands are literal argument arrays beginning with `go`, `test`, with exactly one `-run {test}`, `-run={test}`, `-run ^{test}$` or `-run=^{test}$`. These examples describe separate arguments, not shell strings. Zero or one local scope is allowed: an omitted scope means `.`, and explicit scopes are `.`, `./dir`, `./...` or `./dir/...`, using clean paths. The scope must include the pinned file's package. A recursive scope narrows to that package; it cannot select through `vendor`, `testdata`, dot-prefixed or underscore-prefixed directories, or across another module. Explicit directory scopes must already name the pinned package. File scopes, import paths, multiple scopes, traversal, globbing and other package patterns are refused.

The following flags may each occur once, in any order. Value flags accept either a separate value argument or `=value`; the adapter preserves their spelling, values, order and argument boundaries.

| Flags | Supported values |
| --- | --- |
| `-tags` | Nonempty comma-separated build tags, each containing only letters, digits, `_` or `.` |
| `-timeout` | A nonnegative Go duration, including `0` |
| `-parallel`, `-p`, `-cpu` | One positive integer; CPU lists are refused |
| `-shuffle` | `off`, `on` or a decimal signed 64-bit integer seed |
| `-mod` | `readonly` or `vendor` |
| `-count` | Exactly `1`; absent count becomes `-count=1` to prevent cached execution |
| `-short`, `-v`, `-race`, `-trimpath` | Bare flag, `=true` or `=false` |

Every other flag or command form is refused with a reason, including repeated flags, wrappers and executable paths, partial or repeated placeholders, `-test.*`, `-args`, `--`, benchmarks, fuzzing, listing, JSON reporting, coverage, output paths, compilation-only commands, alternate module files, overlays, tool wrappers and unsupported values. Empty or invisible arguments are refused. Each slash-separated literal selector component is independently regex-escaped and anchored, then supplied as an argument. The display label never selects a test.

An adapter may also implement `RepositoryAcceptanceDiscoverer.Discover(ctx, part, invocation, environment) (RepositoryAcceptanceDiscovery, error)`. The environment optionally implements `RepositoryAcceptanceFileEnvironment`, adding `ReadFile(ctx, partRelativePath) ([]byte, error)` to `Run` and `Close`. Docker reads regular source files and checks their ancestors in the prepared container; paths cannot escape the part or enter `.git`, and archive links and extra entries are refused. Literal Go arguments still run in the owning directory, with the existing offline environment, local toolchain, isolated caches and disposable container. No host caches or extra language runtime are introduced.

Go discovery revalidates the invocation against captured policy, reads and compares the verbatim pinned bytes and their digest, and uses the prepared toolchain's `go list -json` on the narrowed package with the captured build flags (`-tags`, `-p`, `-mod`, `-race`, `-trimpath`). The pinned file must be included under those build constraints. Across `TestGoFiles` and `XTestGoFiles`, the selected name must occur in exactly one top-level function, in the pinned file. It must take one `*testing.T`, with no type parameters or results; renamed and dot imports of `testing` are supported. Methods, other type spellings, wrong imports, malformed sources, excluded files, wrong-file matches and duplicate ordinary or external declarations are refused. `go test -c -o /dev/null` then compiles the narrowed package under the captured policy without running initialization, `TestMain` or tests.

Success returns `RepositoryAcceptanceDiscovery{Binding, TestName}`. Only `Binding.Package` changes, from the preliminary local scope to the actual Go import path. Invocation ID, full identity and source and execution digests remain bound. `TestName` names only the top-level declaration: discovery does not claim that any subtest exists, ran or completed. Every error returns zero discovery and `*RepositoryAcceptanceDiscoveryError{Kind, Reason, Err}`. `Kind` is `refused` for unsupported or mismatched selection/source/declarations, and `setup` for unavailable file-reading capability, tools, invalid package/module discovery, unresolved offline dependencies, compilation failure or interruption. Context errors remain available through `errors.Is`. Direct discovery retains compiler failure as a setup refusal and supplies no acceptance evidence. Configured checks may separately attribute it through the process boundary described below.

The lifecycle validates the returned binding before supplying it to any later runtime collector. Without a collector it retains the discovered package and an unstarted result with a reason. Discovery failures retain their reasons with no package selection or completion claim. Validation consumes the original whole-run allowance; the first suite test or acceptance staging starts the single shared test-phase allowance, and startup, discovery and compilation inherit that same deadline. Private staging, deepest ownership and bounded cleanup remain unchanged. Adapters without `Discover` retain their existing contract.

Default tests exercise real Go processes against standard-library temporary modules with isolated caches, including cancellation, tagged files and external tests. They supplement the existing fake-adapter tests and remain discovery-only. Configured Go admission uses this adapter by default; external plumbing remains unavailable. The local runtime collector below supplies acceptance evidence; the configured compiler attribution boundary below separately investigates completed compiler failures. [D-0321](../decisions/journal/D-0321.jsonl) records the selection and discovery policy, resting on the captured-policy and lifecycle decisions D-0311, D-0314 and D-0316.

## Go lifecycle producer and process transport

[#538's ratified plan](../.invariant/plans/issue-538.json) adds `StartGoAcceptance(ctx, part, test, source, executionDigest, prepare)`. ## Retries

A retry builds a plan's failed draft again, and otherwise watches a pull request that's open, or builds again when there's none ([D-0047](../decisions/journal/D-0047.jsonl), [D-0064](../decisions/journal/D-0064.jsonl), [D-0218](../decisions/journal/D-0218.jsonl)). After a plan's build left a draft, because its agent ended its last run with an error, its tests failed or its review asked for changes, `/invariant retry` has the factory store its record that the retry replaces the draft, close the draft without merging it, so its work stays there to read, and build the ratified plan again from its ratification, on a branch of its own, review included. The new build opens a pull request of its own, ready once its checks and review pass, or a draft again, which a writer can retry in turn ([#271](https://github.com/gitdek/invariant/issues/271)). If the draft's branch, which holds the plan's ratification, is gone, the retry is answered with a note for a person instead: close the draft and comment `/invariant revise` to draft the plan again ([D-0220](../decisions/journal/D-0220.jsonl)). A retry's build on another machine that can't reach the commit holding the ratification says the same once the draft is closed, as a failure for a person, and the dashboard's card for it offers only drafting the plan again. A retry applies to the failure that was the issue's latest when it was posted, so one posted before a draft's failure post, such as a second retry queued while the first draft was open, gets a note and builds nothing ([D-0238](../decisions/journal/D-0238.jsonl)). After CI's gate failed on a pull request, or for a modeled project's draft, or a draft GitHub wouldn't mark ready after its build passed, `/invariant retry` has the factory watch that pull request again. Once CI's gate passes on its current head, it's still in scope with its ratified lock, and GitHub says it can merge it, the factory marks a draft ready and merges it at the head it checked, without running a review ([#145](https://github.com/gitdek/invariant/issues/145), [D-0249](../decisions/journal/D-0249.jsonl), [D-0252](../decisions/journal/D-0252.jsonl)). Its answer to the retry records whether the pull request was a draft or ready, and when the writer retried, and it marks a draft ready only when that answer saw it as a draft and GitHub shows no one has turned it into a draft since the retry. So a person stops a watched pull request by closing it or the issue, or, while it's ready, by turning it into a draft, even before the factory answers the retry: once CI's gate passes, the factory says once that someone turned it into a draft after the retry, and a writer's retry after that has it watch the draft after all. An answer from before #371, which records neither, watches no draft, so a person decides, and so does a draft whose events GitHub won't show, or keeps failing to show for half an hour ([#371](https://github.com/gitdek/invariant/issues/371), [D-0270](../decisions/journal/D-0270.jsonl), [D-0274](../decisions/journal/D-0274.jsonl)). A change to the trusted base waits for a person to merge it instead, and the factory doesn't mark its draft ready ([D-0109](../decisions/journal/D-0109.jsonl)). To change a plan instead, close its pull request, say what to change, comment `/invariant revise`, and ratify the new draft. After a build that stopped before it made a pull request, a retry builds the ratified proposal again. The factory's posts say which happens, and so does the dashboard: a card's retry button says what it does, such as "Build the plan again, replacing #N" or "Merge #N once CI passes", `/act` asks first in the same words and says what follows once it's posted, and the comment it posts is `/invariant retry` either way ([D-0156](../decisions/journal/D-0156.jsonl)). A failed draft's post lists the plan's files in the trusted base in its body, which a post too long for GitHub clips, and keeps its commands and what they do whole ([D-0167](../decisions/journal/D-0167.jsonl)).

## What a build may write

The builder may write the plan's named files and add new regular Go `*_test.go` regression files in packages the plan touches, including the nearest Go package owning a planned embedded asset and new packages the plan names. Staging, formatting, package tests, final copying and CI scope all honor that allowance, alongside the captured non-Go conventions below. Existing unnamed tests, the ratified acceptance tests and plan, other production files and unrelated packages remain protected, and linked files are refused. The original checkout stays the baseline across every repair, so tests the builder adds can later be corrected or removed ([D-0107](../decisions/journal/D-0107.jsonl), [D-0110](../decisions/journal/D-0110.jsonl), [D-0133](../decisions/journal/D-0133.jsonl)). CI's scope check judges a test new against the merge base a pull request's changes come from, so a branch that changes an existing test main has since deleted doesn't count as adding one. Formatting checks only the regression tests the build added, beside the named Go files and acceptance tests, so an existing test the builder may not edit never blocks a build. Package tests count only directories that still hold Go files, so a package the plan deletes isn't tested (#298, [D-0190](../decisions/journal/D-0190.jsonl)).

Under [#654's ratified plan](../.invariant/plans/issue-654.json) and its F1 A choice, captured Python and TypeScript parts admit new regular regression files throughout the whole touched part, including separate test directories and sibling test areas. The deepest owning part bounds the allowance; a nested or unrelated part gains none. Framework-conventional names must also fall within the captured suite's targets and discovery settings. Writable-file discovery, staging, the CLI tool, registered and fresh final checks, review, copying and CI use this same policy. Formatting and suite failures in added tests block review and readiness, including edits after a passing tool run.

Supported discovery is intentionally literal and never executes repository policy:

- Pytest: direct `pytest` or `python3 -m pytest`, default `test_*.py` and `*_test.py` names, literal file/directory targets, `--ignore` and `--ignore-glob`. Part-root `pytest.ini`, `.pytest.ini`, `tox.ini` or `setup.cfg` sections support `testpaths`, `python_files` and `norecursedirs`, including indented INI lists. `pyproject.toml` supports single-line literal strings or string arrays in `tool.pytest.ini_options`. Explicit suite targets take precedence over `testpaths`. Configuration files below the part root close the implicit allowance: a target such as `tests` can make pytest select `tests/pytest.ini`. New `conftest.py` and `__init__.py` files are never implicit regressions; existing collection hooks or package initializers close the implicit allowance.
- Vitest: direct `vitest run`, conventional `.test`/`.spec` JavaScript or TypeScript filenames, optional literal file/directory targets and a part-root `--config`. A default or explicitly selected literal configuration object supports `test.include` and `test.exclude` against part-relative paths. Vitest does not substitute Jest's `<rootDir>` token; patterns containing it grant no implicit additions.
- Jest: direct `jest`, conventional `.test`/`.spec` JavaScript/TypeScript files or files under `__tests__`, optional part-root `--config` and `--runInBand`. Literal `testMatch`, `roots` with `<rootDir>`, and literal path fragments in `testPathIgnorePatterns` are supported. Each `testMatch` pattern must start with `<rootDir>/` or `**/`, so a match inside the part also matches Jest's full path; other forms, including `tests/**/*.test.js`, close the implicit allowance. Configuration may be JSON, a literal `module.exports` or default-export object, or the literal `jest` object in `package.json`. A `babel` property in any captured `package.json` under the part closes the allowance, as do `.babelrc` and `babel.config.*` files, because the default transformer can load executable plugins. Vitest also accepts literal modules. Duplicate keys, executable expressions, imports and later mutations are refused.
- Globs support `*`, `?` and whole-component `**`; unsupported glob or regex dialects do not broaden discovery. Harmless supported verbosity, traceback, color and timeout flags retain their captured argv. Wrappers, test-name filters, plugins, automatic Babel transformations, inherited or nested configuration roots, workspace/project configurations, ambiguous automatic configuration and other unknown options or discovery settings grant no implicit additions. Name patterns always intersect conventional test filenames; they cannot authorize production or configuration lookalikes.

The original checkout supplies both novelty and policy throughout repairs; CI reads both from the diff's merge base, even when the target branch independently adds or removes a test or changes configuration. Symbolic links, hard links and linked ancestors never supply test or copied bytes. Existing unnamed tests, acceptance sources and plan records stay protected. Candidate policy cannot widen permission, and when implicit regressions are present, even named discovery-configuration edits fail checks and scope rather than hiding those tests; a plan changing discovery can explicitly name its new tests. Go keeps its exact-package, new-package, embedded-asset and deleted-package rules and its legacy hashes and locks. External plumbing intake remains disabled.

Mandatory offline regressions cover these discovery refusals and staging against captured policy. `TestPytestOfflineRegressionDiscovery654` and `TestNodeOfflineRegressionDiscovery654` also use the existing Python and Node acceptance-image opt-ins to run the real frameworks: the unsupported configurations hide failing tests or rewrite them through Babel, and supported configurations expose the failures. These opt-ins require the already-local pinned images described above and never install or download tools.

[D-0372](../decisions/journal/D-0372.jsonl) records this step and F1 A, on [D-0173](../decisions/journal/D-0173.jsonl), [D-0190](../decisions/journal/D-0190.jsonl), [D-0301](../decisions/journal/D-0301.jsonl) and [D-0311](../decisions/journal/D-0311.jsonl). ## Time, usage and the provider

The initial build, fallback, repairs and all reviews contribute their turns, cost, token fields and tool calls to the same build's totals, with every transcript and test log retained. They and the factory checks share one execution time allowance; another round or fallback never renews it. Waiting for an agent account's first turn in a run remains excluded; a provider retry's waits, and the turns it takes again, count ([D-0125](../decisions/journal/D-0125.jsonl), [D-0154](../decisions/journal/D-0154.jsonl)).

When an agent's provider is briefly unavailable, at capacity, rate-limited, overloaded, failing on its side after its own retries or dropping the connection, the run tries again in the same workspace after waits of one, two and four minutes: four attempts, about seven minutes for the provider to come back. Every Codex and Claude Code run does, for drafts, plans, builds and reviews. The attempts are one run, with one time limit, and each wait gives Codex's turn back so other steps go on. They share one allowance of gate, check or test calls, which Invariant's MCP server counts before each call against a count the run itself keeps, at an address the run names in each server's configuration, since Codex filters what its servers inherit, and only what's left of Claude Code's budget and turns or Codex's steps. Usage adds up every attempt, and counts a tool call the allowance gave even when the agent's events lost it. A Claude Code attempt that ends after paid replies but before it says what it cost counts the rest of the budget as spent, a conservative estimate, and nothing retries it. Only the provider's own reported failure decides, not earlier stderr or Codex's notices that it's reconnecting, and Claude Code's own fields for the error's kind, status and cause decide before its words; an MCP server that didn't start, a usage limit or other account limit, even one Claude Code reports as a rate limit, and every other failure end the run as before. If the provider stays unavailable, the post quotes the last provider error. A draft proposes nothing, and `/invariant revise` drafting it again is all it needs. A build publishes nothing its agent left, not even a draft pull request, though the leftovers would pass, and `/invariant retry` builds the ratified proposal again, review included ([#261's ratified plan](../.invariant/plans/issue-261.json), [D-0069](../decisions/D-0069-slice-9-plan.md), [D-0138](../decisions/journal/D-0138.jsonl)).

## Plans of issues

New plans of issues have one level under [#560's ratified plan](../.invariant/plans/issue-560.json): every step must draft one complete plumbing or modeled change. Ordinary issues can still propose a plan of issues through a writer's `/invariant plan` or through plumbing planning when the whole issue needs several pull requests. Each plan is ratified by its hash before the factory opens its steps in order ([D-0105](../decisions/D-0105-factory-takes-every-change.md), [D-0147](../decisions/journal/D-0147.jsonl), [D-0162](../decisions/journal/D-0162.jsonl)).

The factory identifies a step from the matching factory record on its parent, checking the issue number, plan hash and step. A copied issue-body marker alone does not establish membership. Membership remains separate from the ratifier's current write permission: another writer's `/invariant plan`, fork answer or revision still drafts one complete change. The parent issue is supplied to planner instructions and check tools. Agent checks and final draft validation refuse `issues.json` even alongside another draft, and the factory also refuses a returned or recovered plan-of-issues result before posting it. Check-tool refusals consume the shared check allowance and are logged as failures.

When the whole step cannot fit one reviewable pull request, the planner asks a fork in `proposal.json`. The question explains what does not fit and suggests replacement steps in the parent. It offers either building the complete step as one change, or having a writer stop the parent with `/invariant stop` and request a replacement plan on a new top-level issue with `/invariant plan`. Stopping the parent prevents further issues from opening; an already-open step goes on as it is. The factory does not rewrite the ratified parent or open replacement steps on its own.

Already-ratified nested plans remain executable under their original hashes, with no build or pull request of their own. A parent waits until the factory records its child's whole plan as complete under the child's ratified hash; an unfinished or stopped child plan holds its parent. Completion carries upward through nested plans on later polls, opening each step and recording each plan's completion once, including after a watcher restarts. Newly drafted leaves of those existing trees must still be one complete change.

## Running the factory

The watcher holds the repository's lease in `refs/invariant/lease`. Under [#544's ratified plan](../.invariant/plans/issue-544.json), `-lease` defaults to fifteen minutes. It checks the lease every poll, including while agents run, and renews once half its duration has gone. Explicit durations still work: an enabled lease must exceed two minutes and cover at least three polls; `-lease 0` disables it ([D-0072](../decisions/journal/D-0072.jsonl)). [D-0324](../decisions/journal/D-0324.jsonl) records this rule for GitHub's failures and the longer default.

A failed lease read or a push error other than `ErrLeaseMoved` keeps only the last confirmed lease. The watcher reports the error and retries renewal on each poll once due; it never pushes without a successful read. The error neither extends the lease nor grants authority before acquisition. Running steps and agent results can still take effects until exactly one minute before the confirmed expiry, by the watcher's own clock. A ref-moved refusal or a read of another holder's unexpired lease stops effects immediately. A renewal from the same commit keeps the running steps' tenure. A push whose answer was lost extends authority only once the watcher reads it back as its own, and a renewal that lands late can still be adopted after a ref-moved refusal, without starting a new tenure ([D-0237](../decisions/journal/D-0237.jsonl), [D-0245](../decisions/journal/D-0245.jsonl)).

Renewals and the release run one at a time, under a lock each holds through its `git` read and push. The lock on what the watcher last confirmed, the lease's expiry and tenure, is never held across a `git` call: a renewal takes it only to read that state before its push and to record the outcome after. So a step's permission check never waits for a renewal's `git`, and checks against the lease last confirmed. A run record's push checks its permission after it takes the clone's lock, which a renewal's `git` commands also take, one at a time, so nothing waits for the clone while it holds the lease state ([#555](https://github.com/gitdek/invariant/issues/555), [D-0339](../decisions/journal/D-0339.jsonl)).

After a crash, another watcher waits until one minute after the lease expires: up to roughly sixteen minutes from the last renewal at the default duration, plus its poll interval. Drain and shutdown still release the lease as soon as steps and renewal have stopped, so a waiting successor can take it on its next poll ([D-0069](../decisions/D-0069-slice-9-plan.md), [D-0072](../decisions/journal/D-0072.jsonl), [D-0307](../decisions/journal/D-0307.jsonl), [D-0312](../decisions/journal/D-0312.jsonl)).

`-parallel` sets the number of agent slots, three by default. Drafting statements, plumbing plans or plans of issues and building modeled projects or plumbing plans all share those slots. An operation holds its slot through its reviews, fallbacks and repairs. Commands, ratifications, merges, closure handling and labels proceed while every slot is occupied, including with `-parallel 1`, and the watcher keeps polling and publishing its heartbeat ([#455's ratified plan](../.invariant/plans/issue-455.json), [D-0300](../decisions/journal/D-0300.jsonl), refining [D-0113](../decisions/D-0113-factory-works-issues-at-once.md)).

Each poll dispatches changed or unfinished issues that have no step running, and returns without waiting for agent capacity. A waiting step still owns its issue across polls, so it cannot start twice. It records and advertises no agent run until it has a slot, then rechecks that the issue is open and the step still belongs to the current lease tenure. A step waiting for a slot reads its issue again every minute, and ends when the issue closes. With a slot, its run is recorded, and its agent may still wait for its turn, as Codex's runs do ([D-0154](../decisions/journal/D-0154.jsonl)). That wait reads the issue every minute too, and the run reads it once more when its turn comes, just before the agent starts. A closed issue ends the step there. No agent starts, and the slot and the turn go to the next step. The step posts nothing but the close any closed issue gets. The run stays recorded, as one that stopped does, so a reopened issue hears that it stopped, and a writer's command starts another. Reviews, fallbacks and repairs after an agent started don't check ([D-0382](../decisions/journal/D-0382.jsonl)). Recorded results can be reused without a slot. Success, error, panic and cancellation release the slot; shutdown joins both queued and running steps. For callers of the Go API, positive `Factory.Parallel` uses this scheduling, while its zero value keeps `Poll` synchronous. Protocol and recovery checks still govern every effect ([D-0069](../decisions/D-0069-slice-9-plan.md), [D-0237](../decisions/journal/D-0237.jsonl)).

Under [#514's ratified plan](../.invariant/plans/issue-514.json), a watcher remembers the issue version a successful step observed, including its comment count, and skips unchanged idle issues. GitHub timestamps have one-second resolution, so recent buckets stay pending until a successful read begins more than one minute after their timestamp. This catches comments and edits made within the same second, even when an earlier read finishes after that overlap. After discovery and this overlap, idle issues add no individual reads: polls make the shared open-issue, closed-issue and recent-merge queries, plus list pagination. Comments, body edits and label changes wake their issues on the next poll. Unanswered commands, ratified builds and unfinished plans of issues, watched pull requests and CI, failed reads or effects, and running or queued steps remain eligible. A 404 reading a listed issue is retried without another update; only an unlisted issue, discovered through a merge or kept for a plan's step, can be settled as gone. A plan's open step issue that the open-issue list leaves out is stepped by its number on every poll until the list names it: GitHub's list once left out an issue the factory had just opened for a step, for 23 minutes, while the issue read as open ([#526](https://github.com/gitdek/invariant/issues/526), [D-0342](../decisions/journal/D-0342.jsonl)). An update listed during a step remains pending for a later step. A new watcher rediscovers work, and every poll publishes its heartbeat, including failed discovery. Missing update timestamps are read conservatively. These are tested polling changes preserving the lease, recovery and closure rules ([D-0069](../decisions/D-0069-slice-9-plan.md), [D-0105](../decisions/D-0105-factory-takes-every-change.md), [D-0113](../decisions/D-0113-factory-works-issues-at-once.md), [D-0237](../decisions/journal/D-0237.jsonl), [D-0299](../decisions/journal/D-0299.jsonl)).

Repeated GitHub REST GETs revalidate with their saved ETags, including each list page, issues, comments, events, pull requests and check runs. A 304 reuses the matching response bytes and decodes fresh objects; each read still contacts GitHub. A changed response replaces both the body and pagination links, while a 304 retains pagination unless GitHub supplies a replacement link. The concurrency-safe cache is isolated by request, representation and authentication, and holds at most 256 responses and 32 MiB. Credentials are resolved for each request; reads without identifiable authentication stay uncached. Errors remain errors, and mutations use no GET validator; the following read revalidates with GitHub. `gh` exits nonzero for every status outside 2xx, 304 included, after printing the response, so a read takes GitHub's status from what `gh` printed; only a run that printed no response is `gh`'s own failure. [D-0318](../decisions/journal/D-0318.jsonl) records the polling decision. This page and SPEC describe the tested behavior under [D-0122](../decisions/journal/D-0122.jsonl) and [D-0294](../decisions/journal/D-0294.jsonl).

`-projects` is the directory new projects go in. `-language` is what the factory writes when an issue doesn't pick one: `go`, `typescript` or `python`. `-agent` is the coding agent that drafts and builds an issue when neither it nor its project picks one: `codex`, the default, or `claude-code`. The watcher runs only the agents `-agents` names, `codex` alone unless you add `claude-code`, so it never starts Claude Code otherwise. Codex runs on `gpt-6-astra` at `ultra`, its highest reasoning (`-codex-model`, `-codex-effort`). The factory acts as you, through `gh`. To have it act as its own bot, [set up a GitHub App](factory-app.md) and add `-app-id`. With Claude Code turned on, each of its runs may spend up to $3 of estimated cost drafting, or reviewing a plan's build, and $5 building, on your account (`-formalize-budget`, `-budget`). A build's fallback, repairs and reviews are runs of their own. A Codex run is capped by its steps (`-codex-steps`). A build whose agent the loop guard stops says why, and runs its agent once more at `-fallback-effort`, `xhigh` by default, when that's below its effort; a fallback that stops the same way fails the build. Claude Code's guard stops a run once two replies in a row end at the output limit with nothing but thinking ([D-0115](../decisions/journal/D-0115.jsonl), [D-0125](../decisions/journal/D-0125.jsonl)). Codex fails its turn itself at the first reply that reaches the model's output limit, saying `Incomplete response returned, reason: max_output_tokens`, and that's its guard's stop, never a provider's failure ([D-0257](../decisions/journal/D-0257.jsonl)). Before it takes an issue, the watcher checks that it can run its coding agent, and any other that `-agents` names and a project's manifest picks: that the agent's CLI runs and is signed in. If one can't run, it doesn't start, and says what to fix. For Codex, sign it in once to a home of its own, which its commands can't read: `invariant/codex` in your user config directory, or `-codex-home`. On macOS:

```bash
mkdir -p "$HOME/Library/Application Support/invariant/codex"
CODEX_HOME="$HOME/Library/Application Support/invariant/codex" codex login
```

Codex's commands then run under a permission profile of Codex's own: they see only the workspace, the system and Go's toolchain, reach no network, and can't read your home directory. The watcher checks that before it runs Codex, and runs one Codex run at a time.

The watcher keeps its clone and every run's transcript, checks and records in its work directory, `-work`, by default `invariant/watch` in your user cache directory. A plan's draft and build each give their checks a Go build cache of their own and remove it when they end, and a watcher removes any a stopped watcher left when it starts ([D-0233](../decisions/journal/D-0233.jsonl)).

## Draining and restarting

Send `SIGUSR1` to the `invariant watch` process to drain it. The command accepts and acknowledges the signal from startup, remembering a request received while setup is still running. Repeated requests are harmless. The Go API's `Factory.Drain()` does the same without waiting for the work to finish, and can be called before `Watch`.

Draining stops admission to new agent operations, including drafts and builds already waiting for an agent slot. Deferred work gets no run record or failure post. Admitted operations finish their checks, reviews, fallbacks, repairs, result recording and publication. While their steps finish, the watcher keeps polling, publishing heartbeats, answering commands, ratifying, merging, handling closure and renewing its lease. Once they finish, it stops dispatching, joins its remaining steps and renewal, releases only its own lease through the existing ten-second cleanup and exits successfully. An idle watcher, including one waiting for another watcher's lease, exits without waiting for its poll timer. A waiting successor can take the released lease on its next poll and continue from the recorded build without running its agent again ([#463's ratified plan](../.invariant/plans/issue-463.json), preserving [D-0069](../decisions/D-0069-slice-9-plan.md), [D-0113](../decisions/D-0113-factory-works-issues-at-once.md), [D-0237](../decisions/journal/D-0237.jsonl) and [D-0300](../decisions/journal/D-0300.jsonl); the decision is [D-0312](../decisions/journal/D-0312.jsonl)).

`SIGTERM`, Ctrl-C (`SIGINT`) and context cancellation still cancel active work, even during a drain. They join the canceled steps and lease renewal before releasing the lease, as [#422's shutdown](../.invariant/plans/issue-422.json) specifies ([D-0307](../decisions/journal/D-0307.jsonl)).

To restart, send the signal to the watcher's PID and wait for that process to exit. If this shell launched it in the background and saved its PID as `watcher_pid`:

```bash
kill -USR1 "$watcher_pid"
wait "$watcher_pid"
```

For a watcher launched elsewhere, wait for its supervisor to report its exit. Only then replace its binary and start the replacement with the same flags and work directory. Admitted runs may still launch checks, reviews or repairs through the current binary while draining, so the acknowledgement alone is not permission to replace it.

Overlapping watchers need separate `-work` directories and an enabled lease. Each directory holds its own clone, worktrees, status and run files; the repository lease does not protect a shared local directory. To stage a successor before the old watcher exits, give it a separate binary as well, then drain the old watcher. The successor waits for the lease and takes it on its next poll after release.

## Recovery

The watcher takes each step as a few effects on GitHub, and it can stop between any two: a crash, a restart, a laptop going to sleep. Two watchers can also run on one repository at once.

Under [#545's ratified plan](../.invariant/plans/issue-545.json), `Clone.Record` and `Clone.Finish` read the run ref after every failed push, even a refusal. The exact commit that call tried to publish means success. A different commit ends the call, with `ErrRecorded` for another writer's record; `Finish` also stops if its ref was deleted, and still rejects a run already finished when the call began. Each `Record` call creates a fresh nonce, so identical descriptions cannot make two watchers claim the same run. Ref updates remain atomic ([D-0069](../decisions/D-0069-slice-9-plan.md), [D-0073](../decisions/journal/D-0073.jsonl)).

Only a read confirming the original expected ref, absent for `Record`, permits another push after a transient failure. The call waits 15, 30, 60 and 120 seconds, for at most five pushes of the same commit with the same force-with-lease expectation. It stops on a refusal, an unreadable or changed ref, cancellation or deadline expiry, loss of the lease or a stale step tenure. Exhaustion returns the last push's error. Every attempt checks the watcher's permission for the same step after acquiring the clone lock, including a resumed run's tenure. Retry waits hold neither the clone nor the lease lock, and waiting for permission releases the clone, so lease renewal can proceed. These are tested watcher rules, preserving the existing resumed-run exception ([D-0113](../decisions/D-0113-factory-works-issues-at-once.md), [D-0237](../decisions/journal/D-0237.jsonl)).

Run pushes use the same GitHub failure classifier as the later-poll retries below. It also recognizes Git's numeric HTTP errors, `fatal error in commit_refs_rules`, timeouts and dropped-connection diagnostics. Explicit refusal statuses, authentication or permission failures, ordinary Git rejections and certificate errors retain precedence over transient words. Other effects keep their retry policies ([D-0265](../decisions/journal/D-0265.jsonl), [D-0266](../decisions/journal/D-0266.jsonl)). [D-0323](../decisions/journal/D-0323.jsonl) records these retries, refining [D-0266](../decisions/journal/D-0266.jsonl); this page, the README and SPEC describe the implemented rules ([D-0122](../decisions/journal/D-0122.jsonl), [D-0294](../decisions/journal/D-0294.jsonl)).

- **The watcher runs on it.** It records each agent run and merge where every watcher can see them, and one watcher at a time holds a lease in a Git ref. It takes every effect only as the proved core allows ([#24](https://github.com/gitdek/invariant/pull/24), [#26](https://github.com/gitdek/invariant/pull/26)). A watcher that takes the lease back after another held it starts a new tenure, and a step it started before then takes no effect, except that an agent run that outlived it still has what it left recorded, its result or why it stopped, for its next step to publish if nothing answered or replaced the build since. The core sees only whether the watcher holds the lease, so the tenure check is the watcher's own, unproved ([D-0237](../decisions/journal/D-0237.jsonl)).
- **GitHub failing isn't GitHub refusing.** When GitHub fails for a moment as the factory marks a draft ready or merges, or reads a watched draft's events before it marks it ready, with a 5xx, a dropped connection, a client's timeout, a name that didn't resolve, a rate limit or gh killed mid-request, the factory asks again on the next poll, where a refusal, such as a 422 or a 405, is said on the issue at once, whatever else its message says. It asks for half an hour from the first failure, and an hour more for GitHub's primary rate limit, which resets within the hour. Then it says once that GitHub kept failing, what it was trying to do and GitHub's last answer, labels the issue for a person, and asks nothing more until a writer's retry has it watch the pull request again. It records when GitHub first failed beside the issue's runs, so the count holds across restarts and watchers. Before it says GitHub kept failing, it reads the pull request again, so a merge GitHub made whose answer was lost is still recorded as the factory's ([#376](https://github.com/gitdek/invariant/issues/376), [D-0265](../decisions/journal/D-0265.jsonl), [D-0266](../decisions/journal/D-0266.jsonl), [D-0267](../decisions/journal/D-0267.jsonl), [D-0274](../decisions/journal/D-0274.jsonl)).
- **A refusal holds the work for a writer.** When GitHub refuses one of a step's effects outright, with a 4xx other than a rate limit or a 408, such as opening a pull request, posting a proposal, opening a plan's issue, changing a label or deleting a branch, the factory records the refusal before it reports it. It quotes the effect and GitHub's own message once on the issue, labels the issue for a person, and does nothing more on it until a writer's new `/invariant retry`. Recorded runs and build results survive, so the retry publishes what was built rather than building again, and a later watcher finishes a report the first couldn't post without repeating the refused effect. Deleting a branch that's already gone isn't refused: GitHub answers that with a 404, or a 422 saying the reference doesn't exist, as after it deletes a merged branch itself ([#434](https://github.com/gitdek/invariant/issues/434), [D-0299](../decisions/journal/D-0299.jsonl)).
- **A closed issue gets its last record.** Closing an issue stops the factory's work on it: it records the close once, leaves an open pull request open, and resumes nothing queued, and a reopened issue picks up where it was. A person's merge of the issue's pull request is recorded once, whether GitHub closed the issue first or the close was recorded before the merge, and a scope check that finds an empty diff reads the pull request again, so a merge made during the check is recorded too. Closed-issue discovery uses one paginated query, filtering active factory labels locally. Startup scans all closed history for unfinished work; later polls query updates since one minute before the previous successful poll began. Failed polls leave that watermark unchanged, and discovered work stays eligible until its step succeeds, even after it falls outside the query window. Merge discovery independently reads the 30 closed pull requests updated most recently and wakes the issue of each factory merge not yet observed successfully, even when its issue timestamp did not change ([#434](https://github.com/gitdek/invariant/issues/434), [D-0299](../decisions/journal/D-0299.jsonl)).
- **A retry can replace a failed draft.** For [#271](https://github.com/gitdek/invariant/issues/271), a coding agent amended the recovery model by hand, and @gitdek ratified it as [D-0203](../decisions/journal/D-0203.jsonl), and its exact proposal as [D-0210](../decisions/journal/D-0210.jsonl). Each writer's retry starts a new build, with its own agent run, which replaces the failed draft. The factory stores its record that the retry replaces the draft before it closes it, at most one of the work's pull requests is open at a time, and each build opens at most one. Each build is an attempt with its number, so the core itself refuses to count a run an earlier attempt started for the latest build ([D-0212](../decisions/journal/D-0212.jsonl)). TLC checks it in 29,725 states, for any number of retries, and Gobra proved the core again, 46 of 46 functions. A modeled project's failed draft, which a retry only watches again, was a ready pull request in the model's terms until #354 ([D-0211](../decisions/journal/D-0211.jsonl)). The watcher maps its retries onto it, and a test stops the watcher before each of a rebuild's 16 effects, from the retry's record to the merge, then starts a fresh one here or on another machine: every time, the draft is replaced once, with one successor, one build per retry and one merge ([D-0218](../decisions/journal/D-0218.jsonl)). Since [#354](https://github.com/gitdek/invariant/issues/354), the model also covers a writer's retry that watches a modeled project's failed draft again, which the factory marks ready and merges once CI's gate passes and GitHub says it can merge it, unless it changes the trusted base, ratified by hand for its exact proposal as [D-0225](../decisions/journal/D-0225.jsonl). TLC checks it in 50,941 states, and Gobra proves the core, 48 of 48 functions. The watcher maps each pull request as GitHub has it, a draft as a draft, and marks ready only a draft a retry watched as that draft, merging it only at the head whose gate passed. A draft a person made of the factory's pull request stays theirs, whether they made it before or after a retry watched the pull request: the factory says so once, and a writer's retry has it watch the draft after all ([D-0232](../decisions/journal/D-0232.jsonl), [D-0249](../decisions/journal/D-0249.jsonl), [D-0252](../decisions/journal/D-0252.jsonl), [D-0270](../decisions/journal/D-0270.jsonl)).

## How its rules changed

- **A person ratified the next change by hand.** [#271](https://github.com/gitdek/invariant/issues/271) asks that a writer's retry build a failed plumbing build again and replace its draft. A coding agent drafted the protocol's `Rebuild` step, with two invariants, three witnesses and two known bugs, checked by TLC in 25,206 states, and @gitdek ratified it as a decision, [D-0202](../decisions/journal/D-0202.jsonl). Gobra proved the core again, 22 of 22 functions. A lock ratified that way holds only for the exact proposal a person's ratification in the journal names, as one ratified on an issue holds only for the hash its comment names: CI, `invariant decisions check`, the gate and the dashboard all check it, so the lock named [D-0209](../decisions/journal/D-0209.jsonl), which ratifies the amendment's exact proposal ([D-0204](../decisions/journal/D-0204.jsonl), [D-0208](../decisions/journal/D-0208.jsonl)), until [#428](https://github.com/gitdek/invariant/issues/428) amended the protocol on its issue to authorize the follow-up that records a merged plan's decision ([D-0293](../decisions/journal/D-0293.jsonl)). The journal records a person's ratification; CI checks that record, and can't prove the person made it, as it can a comment on GitHub. Since #271's plumbing, the watcher takes it: a writer's retry of a plan's failed draft closes the draft and builds the plan again, in a pull request that replaces it ([D-0218](../decisions/journal/D-0218.jsonl)).
